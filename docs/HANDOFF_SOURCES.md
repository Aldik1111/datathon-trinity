# Regulatory and model-source audit (ForteBank Trinity)

**Scope and review date:** bounded source review for episode labels and model provenance, checked 18 October 2026. This is a source map for human review, not a legal opinion, audit finding about ForteBank, or determination that a particular employee or client breached a rule. The official Adilet index reported documents current through 31 August 2026 when checked; this audit does not certify later amendments or the legal force of every provision for a particular transaction.

## What the repository supports

`configs/rules.json` is intentionally an empty list. The existing scenario documentation says table labels are weak, some stated causes are disputed, and visual scores are candidates for manual review. The labels and implementation in `src/training/scenario_dataset.py` likewise encode observable-looking hypotheses, not verified legal elements. No public source reviewed here supports adding any of the 18 labels as a verified episode-level violation rule. The empty registry should remain empty until the bank's applicable internal procedure, transaction records, and the complete episode are available and a qualified reviewer approves a rule mapping.

The most relevant public instrument located is the National Bank's Rules for cash operations and collection, Resolution No. 231 of 29 November 2019, registered as No. 19680. The consolidated Adilet text contains amendments dated November 2025 and March 2026; it defines cash acceptance, issue, counting and storage, links cash receipt/issue to AML due-diligence measures, requires staff responsibility and duty allocation, and describes records and storage controls. See paragraphs 3, 7, 13–16, 64–65 and 74. Paragraph 74's current storage wording was amended in March 2026 and its note states an effective date of 12 July 2026. These provisions are useful control context. They do not let a viewer infer from a few frames that cash had or had not been recorded in the bank system, that a particular item was entrusted bank cash, or that an internal procedure was breached.

The AML/CFT law's current Adilet text provides customer due-diligence requirements (notably Articles 5 and 7). The cash-operations Rules' paragraph 7 expressly refers to due diligence under that law before cash receipt and issue. This is a requirement to perform the prescribed process, not a requirement that a paper identity document must be visible in the camera view. A previously completed, electronic, remote, or otherwise non-visible check cannot be ruled out from this footage. Nor can the camera establish whether a customer was already identified, the transaction type/risk, or which statutory measures applied.

## Episode labels: observation versus absent evidence versus legal conclusion

Use three distinct report fields: **visible observation** (what the sampled image/video shows), **not observed in this episode/view** (an act, record, identity, or status was not visible), and **legal qualification** (unresolved). Avoid converting “not observed” to “not performed” or “violation.” In particular:

| Existing label family | Defensible visual statement | What the episode alone cannot establish | Regulatory connection / disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| `money_returned` | An apparent hand-to-hand transfer of cash may be visible. | That cash was actually returned to a client, who owned it, why it moved, or whether it was correctly posted/receipted. | Cash receipt/issue is within Rule 3(1). **A return/issue of cash alone is not a violation.** Check transaction and cashier records; no verified mapping. |
| `identity_check_missing` | No document-checking action was visible in the sampled frames, if that is what the clip shows. | Whether identity was checked earlier, electronically, outside frame, under a different lawful procedure, or whether a fresh check was required for this operation. | Rules 7 and AML/CFT Law Arts. 5, 7 are relevant process controls. A camera-only absence cannot establish failure to identify. No verified mapping. |
| `cash_count_missing`, `report_record_missing` | Counting or report-writing was not visible in the observed interval. | Whether counting/recording occurred off-camera, before/after the clip, electronically, or in another system. Day-end documentation and checks required by Rule 64–65 are not proof of a per-client visual sequence. | Compare the cash document, ledger/system event and applicable internal process. “Not seen” is not “not done.” No verified mapping. |
| `money_not_secured`, `money_not_secured_before_client_left` | Cash-like objects appear outside a visible safe/room for a visible interval; if the customer's departure is visible, record that separately. | Whether these are bank/customer funds, counted or posted, in an authorized intermediate stage, subject to another control, or in fact left unsecured; frame order does not prove transaction completion or custodial responsibility. | Rule 74 requires storage of cash in vault and/or safe room after accounting in the automated digital system, in accordance with the bank's internal documents. This sequence makes accounting state and internal procedure essential facts. It does **not** justify collapsing “cash outside a visible safe,” “safe open,” and “client left” into one proven violation. No verified mapping. |
| `safe_left_open`, `safe_key_left` | The safe door appears open; a key-like object appears in or near it, if visually clear. | Whether the safe was in use, empty, secured by another control, the object is a key, or whether a cash-storage duty had begun. | Rule 74 covers cash storage after system accounting; it does not establish that every visible open door/key position is itself a breach. Check the relevant internal security procedure and custody state. No verified mapping. |
| `cash_bag_unattended`, `protected_items_unsecured` | A bag/object is visible without a person in the sampled view. | Contents, ownership, whether it contains cash/valuables, whether it is attended outside frame, or whether it is in an authorized controlled location. | Cash collection/storage Rules may be relevant only after contents, custody and process are established. Do not infer “unattended bank cash” from appearance. No verified mapping. |
| `parallel_service`, `client_left_before_count_complete`, `mixed_client_cash`, `mixed_denominations` | Multiple persons/scenes, apparent overlapping service, apparent movement between groups, or visually different notes may be described cautiously. | Whether these are different clients, separate/merged transactions, denominations/currencies, or an incomplete count; edits and sparse samples can create false sequence. | Cash Rules regulate particular cash operations and controls, not these visual proxies as named violations. Confirm identities only through authorized records and assess internal procedures. No verified mapping. |
| `unattended_screen` | A lit display is visible without a staff member in the view. | Whether it is a bank workstation, whether a session is authenticated or sensitive data displayed, whether staff are just outside frame, or whether it is locked. | No episode-specific public rule mapping was verified. Treat as an operational observation for internal review, not a legal conclusion. |
| `door_left_open`, `entry_through_open_door` | A doorway appears open; a person appears to pass through it. | Door's purpose, access authorization, whether it is a controlled cash-room boundary, alarm/lock state, or whether access was permitted. | The separate premises Rules require a cash-room node and its isolation/guarding for the specified premises (paras. 3, 6–8), but the episode cannot establish room category, authorized access, or a breach of those conditions. No verified episode-level mapping. |
| `personal_items_before_shift` | An object that may be personal is visible in the scene. | Ownership, whether it was personal property, shift timing, or whether the relevant restricted area/policy applies. | No public rule identified that makes this visual label a violation by itself. Requires bank policy and verified context. No verified mapping. |

These dispositions deliberately distinguish generic cash security controls from particular visual cues. If a reviewer later has the required non-video facts, the report should cite the exact applicable internal document/version and transaction record; public cash Rules alone do not supply all episode-specific elements.

## Model and weights: separate provenance checks

The code identifies the frozen feature backbone as the ONNX Model Zoo file `mobilenetv2-12.onnx` and pins SHA-256 `c0c3f76d93fa3fd6580652a45618618a220fced18babf65774ed169de0432ad5` in `src/vision/scenario_features.py`. The official ONNX Model Zoo MobileNet page describes its classification purpose, ImageNet input normalization and lists “Apache 2.0” under License. The ONNX Model Zoo repository also has a root Apache-2.0 license. This supports recording **the model-zoo entry's stated license as Apache 2.0**, subject to matching the actual distributed model file and retaining the applicable license/NOTICE terms.

The repository currently contains no checked-in backbone/model artifact in this worktree (artifacts are local/ignored); this source audit did not certify a deployed file's license or legal rights. The local preparation run records the actual archived `backbone.onnx` SHA-256 in ignored `artifacts/handoff-input/integrity.json`; the model's pinned checksum and runtime-loader acceptance are technical integrity evidence, not a license opinion or legal certification. Before distribution, match the file hash to the pinned code, preserve the exact upstream LICENSE and any required NOTICE/attribution materials, and recheck the model card/file provenance. Do not infer that Apache licensing of ONNX code, the Model Zoo repository, or an entry automatically licenses the original training dataset, all upstream source materials, or this project's videos/annotations. The ONNX README identifies ImageNet as the training/validation dataset but this audit did not review the dataset's terms. The learned scenario heads and user-provided examples have separate provenance and rights questions; none are resolved by the backbone license.
